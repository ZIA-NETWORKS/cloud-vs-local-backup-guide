# Cloud Backup vs Local Backup: A Guide for Small Businesses

A plain-English guide to choosing between **cloud backup vs local backup**, with a simple rule to follow and a checklist you can copy. Written for small businesses, including those in places like Santa Fe, New Mexico, where monsoon power surges, wildfire season, and dry, dusty conditions are real concerns.

> This guide is general information, not legal or compliance advice. If you handle health, financial, or other regulated data, confirm which rules apply to you.

## The short answer

For most small businesses, use **both**. Local backup gives you fast recovery for everyday problems. Cloud backup protects you when something happens to your whole location. Each one covers the other's weak spot.

## Local backup

Copies of your files stored on hardware you own, such as an external drive, a network storage device, or a small server.

**Strengths**
- Fast restores
- Works without internet
- One-time hardware cost instead of a monthly fee

**Weaknesses**
- Stored in the same place as your computers, so fire, flood, theft, or a power surge can destroy both
- Hardware wears out and needs checking
- Easy to forget to run or test

## Cloud backup

Copies of your files sent over the internet to a provider's secure data center.

**Strengths**
- Offsite by default
- Runs automatically
- Scales as your data grows
- Covers remote and traveling staff

**Weaknesses**
- First backup can take days on a slow connection
- Large restores depend on your internet speed
- Ongoing subscription cost
- You rely on a vendor's security and support

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Local backup | Cloud backup |
|---|---|---|
| Restore speed | Fast | Depends on internet |
| Protects against site-wide disaster | No | Yes |
| Works without internet | Yes | No |
| Ongoing cost | Low | Monthly or yearly fee |
| Setup effort | Moderate | Low |
| Needs manual upkeep | Often | Rarely |
| Best for | Quick recovery | Disaster protection |

## The 3-2-1 rule

- **3** copies of your data (the original plus two backups)
- **2** different types of storage (for example, a local drive and the cloud)
- **1** copy kept offsite

## Risks to plan for in northern New Mexico

- **Monsoon storms:** lightning and power surges can damage computers and plugged-in drives
- **Wildfire season:** you may need to leave your building quickly and still reach your files
- **Dry air and dust:** hard on electronics over time
- **Busy seasons:** little time for manual backup routines, so automation matters
- **Older buildings:** wiring and climate control may not suit servers or storage devices

These all point toward keeping at least one copy away from your office.

## Backup checklist

- [ ] I know where all my important business data lives
- [ ] I have at least 3 copies of it
- [ ] I use at least 2 different types of storage
- [ ] At least 1 copy is offsite
- [ ] My backups are encrypted in storage and in transit
- [ ] I use versioning or immutable copies to protect against ransomware
- [ ] Power surge protection is in place for local devices
- [ ] I have tested a restore in the last 6 months
- [ ] My recovery plan is written down (who does what, where credentials are kept)
- [ ] I review this setup at least once a year
